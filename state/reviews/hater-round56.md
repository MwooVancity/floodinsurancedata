## louisiana
Text identical on this page (checked in the file). Sources read in raw text: FEMA fact sheet (March 2023) extracted with pypdf; eCFR 44 CFR part 61 as of 2026-10-01.
- "Hurricanes can cause both wind and flood damage": fact sheet "hurricanes, tropical storms and nor'easters can cause wind and floodwater damage". Supported.
- "does not pay for wind damage": 44 CFR 61 exclusion list, "8. Theft, fire, explosion, wind, or windstorm" (dwelling form text; also in the other forms). Scope "standard NFIP policy" right.
- "rain blown in by wind": fact sheet "if rain is propelled into a covered structure by wind, that is considered wind-driven rain and is not covered under your flood insurance policy". Supported.
- "water coming through a roof the wind damaged": fact sheet "if your roof is damaged and water enters through the ceiling. That is water damage as a result of wind damage and is not covered". Supported (page adds "wind" to the roof damage; the source's own conclusion says it is wind damage).
- "does pay for water entering from the ground up, such as storm surge": fact sheet "covers damage caused by water entering your home from the ground up due to storm surge, heavy rainfall or the overflow of a body of water". Supported; strength kept.
- "Other policies, such as homeowners policies, may cover wind-driven rain": fact sheet "do not cover water damage from flooding but may cover damage from other perils, such as wind-driven rain or fire". "may" kept.
- "you will likely file claims with both": fact sheet "You will likely need to file insurance claims with both entities". "likely" kept.
No contradiction with the flood-definition sentence or the not-covered list next to it. A reader is not led to think storm surge is excluded or that homeowners surely cover wind.
MINOR 1: "both" at the end has a loose antecedent (could read as wind and flood damage, not the two policies); "with both policies" would be clearer.
MINOR 2: "such as storm surge" omits that heavy rain and river overflow also count when water enters from the ground up; a reader flooded by rain runoff might wonder (fact sheet lists them). Also assumes the reader has a homeowners policy.
Verdict: PASS
