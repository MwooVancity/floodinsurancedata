How we check them Three things to know first How fast does it start? Most policies start 30 days after you buy, but there is no wait if you buy it with a new or refinanced mortgage. Am I required to buy it? Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside them, a lender can still require it. In Florida, Citizens homes with wind cover need it in a high-risk zone since 2023; outside one, since January 2026 if they cost $400,000 or more to rebuild, and from January 2027 for all others. Check your zone on FEMA's flood map . Does my homeowners policy cover floods? Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage; flood insurance is bought separately, through the federal NFIP or a private insurer. Is my quote normal? Type your yearly cost (premium plus fees) to see where it sits among U.S. single-family policies; your state page compares it with your area. What sets the price, and how do I pay less? FEMA prices each building from where it is (distance to the coast, rivers and other flood sources, and how high the ground sits), how it is built (foundation type, first-floor height) and what the policy covers (coverage amounts and deductibles). FEMA's ways to lower an NFIP bill: a higher deductible (you pay more of any loss), work that lowers the building's flood risk, and an Elevation Certificate. The usual building deductible on U.S. single-family policies is $1,250 (51.7% of them); your state page lists the mix there and any community discount. Why do sites quote different prices? Because they measure different things: each row below is the same FEMA records counted another way.
