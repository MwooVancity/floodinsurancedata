Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside them, a lender can still require it. A private flood policy can meet a lender's flood requirement: federally regulated lenders must accept a private policy that meets the federal definition of private flood insurance and covers at least what the loan requires (12 CFR §339.3(c)). Its price is not in FEMA's records. 57.7% of Galveston County's NFIP policy units are in high-risk zones (not 57.7% of all buildings, and not an answer about your own home). Waiting period 30 days Bought with a new or refinanced mortgage No wait NFIP building limit $250,000 NFIP contents limit $100,000 Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage; flood insurance is bought separately, through the federal NFIP or a private insurer. Exceptions: no wait if you buy while making, increasing, extending or renewing a mortgage; a one-day wait if a map revision puts the property in a high-risk zone and you buy within 13 months of the revision (44 CFR §61.11). To buy: call the NFIP at (877) 336-2627 , go to FloodSmart.gov , or ask any licensed insurance agent. Every NFIP insurer offers the same rates. Already flooded? Insured: report the loss to your insurance agent or company now (FEMA's steps: how to start a flood claim ), and send a proof of loss (your signed, sworn statement of the amount you are claiming) within 60 days of the flood (44 CFR 61, App. A(1), Art. VII). Not insured: a policy bought today will not cover a flood that already happened, and FEMA disaster aid is open to people in counties designated in a federally declared disaster. If FEMA aid pays to repair a home in a high-risk zone, you must buy and keep flood insurance on it, or you cannot get that aid again after a later flood.
