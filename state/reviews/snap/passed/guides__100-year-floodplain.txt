What is a 100-year floodplain? FEMA's definition, and the share of NFIP policy units rated in those zones, by state Flood Insurance Data What is a 100-year floodplain? In FEMA's rules, the land in the flood plain with a 1 percent or greater chance of flooding in any given year is the special flood hazard area. The flood with a one percent chance of being equalled or exceeded in any given year is the base flood, also called the 100-year flood (44 CFR §59.1). In FEMA's records, 62.9% of the 4,529,364 NFIP policy units in force in 50 states and DC are rated in zones A or V, the zones FEMA's map uses for that area. 62.9% of 4,529,364 NFIP policy units in force: rated in zones A or V FEMA OpenFEMA NFIP Policies v3, as of August 31, 2026. A unit is FEMA's policyCount on each policy, summed. Each policy is counted by the zone it is rated in, which can differ from the zone on today's map. Buildings without NFIP flood insurance are not in these counts. Is my building in one? On FEMA's flood insurance rate map, the special flood hazard area usually shows as zones A, AO, AH, A1-30, AE, A99, AR, VO, V1-30, VE or V (44 CFR §59.1). To see the zone FEMA's map shows at your building, put the pin on it in the flood zone map . How likely is the 100-year flood? A 1 percent or greater chance a year works out to a 26% or greater chance of at least one such flood over 30 years. FEMA's own figure: over the 10 years 2014 to 2024, nearly one-third (29%) of NFIP flood insurance claims came from areas outside of current high-risk flood areas. Am I required to buy flood insurance there? Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. Floodplain in the wider sense The same rules use flood plain more widely: any land area that can be flooded by water from any source (44 CFR §59.1).
