How we check them Or look up a ZIP code . What to know first How fast does it start? Most policies start 30 days after you buy. Bought while making, increasing, extending or renewing a mortgage, it can start at closing if the insurer is paid within 10 days (30 if the lender, title company or settlement attorney pays). Am I required to buy it? Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. In Florida, homes insured by Citizens (the state-backed insurer) with wind cover need it in a high-risk zone since 2023; outside one, since January 2026 if they cost $400,000 or more to rebuild, and from January 2027 for all others. Check your zone on FEMA's flood map or with the flood zone lookup . Does my homeowners policy cover floods? Most homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage. Where do I buy it? To buy: call the NFIP at (877) 336-2627 , go to FloodSmart.gov , or ask any licensed insurance agent. Every NFIP insurer offers the same rates. A private flood policy can meet a lender's flood requirement: federally regulated lenders must accept a private policy that meets the federal definition of private flood insurance and covers at least what the loan requires (12 CFR §339.3(c)). Its price is not in FEMA's records. How much can it pay? For a home, NFIP building coverage goes up to $250,000 and contents coverage up to $100,000 ; a renter's contents coverage goes up to $100,000 ; for a business, building and contents each go up to $500,000 . Type your yearly cost (premium plus fees) to see where it sits among U.S. single-family policies; your state page compares it with your area. What sets the price, and how do I pay less? FEMA prices each building from where it is (distance to the coast, rivers and other flood sources, and how high the ground sits), how it is built (foundation type, first-floor height) and what the policy covers (coverage amounts and deductibles). FEMA's ways that may lower an NFIP bill: a higher deductible (you pay more of any loss), work that lowers the building's flood risk, and an Elevation Certificate. The usual building deductible on U.S. single-family policies is $1,250 (51.6% of them); your state page lists the mix there and any community discount. Why do sites quote different prices?
