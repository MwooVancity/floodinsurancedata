Did FEMA aid homeowners after floods and storms? Are these counts complete? Am I required to buy it? Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. In Florida, a home insured by Citizens Property Insurance with wind coverage must also carry flood insurance. In a high-risk zone (A or V) this has applied since 2023 (new policies from April 1, renewals from July 1), whatever the home is worth. Outside those zones it phases in by rebuilding cost: from January 1, 2026 at $400,000 or more, and from January 1, 2027 for all other Citizens homes. Condo-unit and no-wind policies are exempt. Citizens does not sell flood insurance: the required flood policy comes from another insurer, the NFIP or a private one, and must at least match the NFIP's dwelling coverage or meet Florida's private flood insurance rules (s. 627.715). At a closing: In Florida, a home seller must give the buyer a flood disclosure at or before the sales contract, saying whether the seller knows of flood damage during their ownership, filed a flood claim, or received flood aid (s. 689.302, F.S.). Only disasters counted in the flood-disaster card are included; their aid can still cover wind or tornado damage from the same storm. Disasters for COVID, fire, winter storms or tornadoes alone are left out. Source: FEMA OpenFEMA — Housing Assistance (Owners). Data: homeowner aid by disaster; retrieved November 14, 2026.
