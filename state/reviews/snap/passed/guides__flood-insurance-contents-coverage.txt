What does flood insurance cover? Building and contents coverage, with FEMA's policy records by state Flood Insurance Data What does flood insurance cover? NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) flood insurance has two parts: building coverage, for physical damage to the insured building, and contents coverage, for your belongings inside it. They are typically bought separately and have separate deductibles. In FEMA's records, 81% of the 2,320,412 U.S. single-family primary-home NFIP policies carry some contents coverage. 81% of 2,320,412 U.S. NFIP single-family primary-home policies: some contents coverage 77% of all 2,320,412 U.S. NFIP single-family primary-home policies: building coverage of $250,000 or more FEMA OpenFEMA NFIP Policies v3, as of August 31, 2026. A policy counts for contents when its contents coverage is above $0. The building figure counts policies whose building coverage (totalBuildingInsuranceCoverage) is $250,000 or more, the most a home can buy. The shares count policies, not homes. What building coverage pays for FloodSmart lists what building coverage pays for: electrical and plumbing systems; furnaces and water heaters; refrigerators, stoves and built-in appliances such as dishwashers; permanently installed carpeting, cabinets, paneling and bookcases; window blinds; foundation walls, anchorage systems and staircases; detached garages; and fuel tanks, well water tanks and pumps, and solar energy equipment. For a home, building coverage goes up to $250,000 . Is contents coverage part of a flood policy? NFIP building coverage and contents coverage are bought separately. The NFIP's standard home policy covers your belongings only if you bought contents coverage, which the policy calls personal property coverage (44 CFR §61.3 and Part 61, Appendix A(1)). Most NFIP policies take effect 30 days after you buy , so a policy bought today will not cover a flood this week. What contents coverage pays for FloodSmart lists what contents coverage pays for: clothing, furniture and electronic equipment; curtains; a clothes washer and dryer; portable and window air conditioners; a microwave; carpet installed over wood floors; and valuable items such as original artwork and furs, up to $2,500. For a home, contents coverage goes up to $100,000 . Belongings in a basement A basement is a floor below ground level on all sides. In a basement, in any flood zone, contents coverage pays for only a short list: portable or window air conditioners, clothes washers and dryers, and food freezers (not walk-in) with the food in them. Furniture, clothing and other belongings kept there are not covered by contents coverage. Each item must be installed where it works and, if needed, connected to power. The same short list applies below the lowest elevated floor of an elevated building in FEMA zones A1-A30, AE, AH, AR, V1-V30 or VE. That holds only if the building was built or substantially improved after 1974, or after the area's first flood insurance rate map took effect if that was later (Dwelling Form, 44 CFR Part 61, Appendix A(1)). What flood insurance does not cover The policy generally counts a flood only when water covers two or more acres of normally dry land or two or more properties, one of them yours. The NFIP's standard policy for homes (the Dwelling Form) does not pay for a hotel or other living costs while you are out of the home, or for belongings outside the building, and it limits what it pays for in a basement. Belongings are paid at actual cash value (their used value), not the cost of new ones.
