Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside them, a lender can still require it. In Florida, a home insured by Citizens Property Insurance with wind coverage must also carry flood insurance. In a high-risk zone (A or V) this has applied since 2023 (new policies from April 1, renewals from July 1), whatever the home is worth. Outside those zones it phases in by rebuilding cost: from January 1, 2026 at $400,000 or more, and from January 1, 2027 for all other Citizens homes. Condo-unit and no-wind policies are exempt. A private flood policy can meet a lender's flood requirement: federally regulated lenders must accept a private policy that meets the federal definition of private flood insurance and covers at least what the loan requires (12 CFR §339.3(c)). Its price is not in FEMA's records. 89.5% of Miami-Dade County's NFIP policy units are in high-risk zones (not 89.5% of all buildings, and not an answer about your own home). Insured: report the loss to your insurance agent or company now (FEMA's steps: how to start a flood claim ), and send a proof of loss (your signed, sworn statement of the amount you are claiming) within 60 days of the flood (44 CFR 61, App. A(1), Art. VII). Not insured: a policy bought today will not cover a flood that already happened, and FEMA disaster aid is open to people in counties designated in a federally declared disaster. If FEMA aid pays to repair a home in a high-risk zone, you must buy and keep flood insurance on it, or you cannot get that aid again after a later flood.
