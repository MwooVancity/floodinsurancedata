Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. In Florida, a home insured by Citizens Property Insurance with wind coverage must also carry flood insurance. In a high-risk zone (A or V) this has applied since 2023 (new policies from April 1, renewals from July 1), whatever the home is worth. Outside those zones it phases in by rebuilding cost: from January 1, 2026 at $400,000 or more, and from January 1, 2027 for all other Citizens homes. Condo-unit and no-wind policies are exempt. Citizens does not sell flood insurance: the required flood policy comes from another insurer, the NFIP or a private one, and must at least match the NFIP's dwelling coverage or meet Florida's private flood insurance rules (s. 627.715). At a closing: In Florida, a home seller must give the buyer a flood disclosure at or before the sales contract, saying whether the seller knows of flood damage during their ownership, filed a flood claim, or received flood aid (s. 689.302, F.S.). 42.1% of Palm Beach County's NFIP policy units are in high-risk zones (not 42.1% of all buildings, and not an answer about your own home). Work out when a new NFIP policy, or added or increased NFIP coverage, starts. Without an exception, the earliest start is 12:01 a.m. local time on the 30th calendar day after the application date, if the insurer receives the form and full amount due within 10 calendar days of that date or they are mailed by certified mail within 4 calendar days; otherwise it is the 30th calendar day after the insurer receives them. With the map exception the same receipt rules apply, but the start is the next calendar day instead of the 30th. The map exception holds only if the insurer receives the form and full amount due within 13 months of the map revision's effective date; after that, the 30-day wait applies. With the loan exception, coverage starts at the time of the loan closing if the insurer receives the form and full amount due within 30 calendar days of closing when the lender, title company or settlement attorney pays, or within 10 calendar days when you or anyone else pays; otherwise the earliest start is the 30th calendar day after the insurer receives them. Contents-only coverage qualifies only if the contents are part of the security for the loan.
