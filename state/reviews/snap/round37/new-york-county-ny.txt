Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside them, a lender can still require it. A private flood policy can meet a lender's flood requirement: federally regulated lenders must accept a private policy that meets the federal definition of private flood insurance and covers at least what the loan requires (12 CFR §339.3(c)). Its price is not in FEMA's records. 43.6% of New York County's NFIP policy units are in high-risk zones (not 43.6% of all buildings, and not an answer about your own home). Most policies start 30 days after you buy, but there is no wait if you buy it with a new or refinanced mortgage. Waiting period 30 days Bought with a new or refinanced mortgage No wait NFIP building limit $250,000 NFIP contents limit $100,000 Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage; flood insurance is bought separately, through the federal NFIP or a private insurer. Exceptions: no wait if you buy while making, increasing, extending or renewing a mortgage; a one-day wait if a map revision puts the property in a high-risk zone and you buy within 13 months of the revision (44 CFR §61.11). To buy: call the NFIP at (877) 336-2627 , go to FloodSmart.gov , or ask any licensed insurance agent. Every NFIP insurer offers the same rates. Congress has to keep renewing the NFIP's authority. During a lapse, NFIP policies cannot be bought, renewed or increased until the authority returns. Policies already in force run to their expiration date and claims on them are still handled, though FEMA says the money to pay claims may be limited. Already flooded? Insured: report the loss to your insurance agent or company now (FEMA's steps: how to start a flood claim ), and send a proof of loss (your signed, sworn statement of the amount you are claiming) within 60 days of the flood (44 CFR 61, App. A(1), Art. VII). Not insured: a policy bought today will not cover a flood that already happened, and FEMA disaster aid is open to people in counties designated in a federally declared disaster. If FEMA aid pays to repair a home in a high-risk zone, you must buy and keep flood insurance on it, or you cannot get that aid again after a later flood.
