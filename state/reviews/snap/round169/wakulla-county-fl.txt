Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. In Florida, a home insured by Citizens Property Insurance with wind coverage must also carry flood insurance. In a high-risk zone (A or V) this has applied since 2023 (new policies from April 1, renewals from July 1), whatever the home is worth. Outside those zones it phases in by rebuilding cost: from January 1, 2026 at $400,000 or more, and from January 1, 2027 for all other Citizens homes. Condo-unit and no-wind policies are exempt. Citizens does not sell flood insurance: the required flood policy comes from another insurer, the NFIP or a private one, and must at least match the NFIP's dwelling coverage or meet Florida's private flood insurance rules (s. 627.715). At a closing: In Florida, a home seller must give the buyer a flood disclosure at or before the sales contract, saying whether the seller knows of flood damage during their ownership, filed a flood claim, or received flood aid (s. 689.302, F.S.). 87.5% of Wakulla County's NFIP policy units are in high-risk zones (not 87.5% of all buildings, and not an answer about your own home).
