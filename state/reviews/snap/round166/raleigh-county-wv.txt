Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. 71.3% of Raleigh County's NFIP policy units are in high-risk zones (not 71.3% of all buildings, and not an answer about your own home). How this is counted What each means: zone AE , zone X , zone VE . What the zone codes mean (44 CFR §64.3): A, AE and A1–A30 are high-risk; AO and AH are high-risk with shallow flooding; A99 is high-risk behind a flood barrier FEMA treats as complete; V, VE and V1–V30 are coastal high-risk, flooded by tides with fast-moving water (velocity); B and X are areas of moderate flood hazard, C and X areas of minimal hazard; D has possible but undetermined flood hazards. Inland flood risk rating (FEMA) Relatively Moderate FEMA's rating weighs modeled loss with social vulnerability and community resilience; it is not a prediction for your home, and it can differ from the loss percentile below.
