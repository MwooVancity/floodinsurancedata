A renter policy here is one where FEMA marks the policyholder a tenant and the building residential, with no building coverage and some contents coverage. Priced means a policy cost above $0. How we check them . What does renters flood insurance cover? An NFIP renters policy protects the things you own inside your home, such as furniture, clothes, televisions, computers, rugs and some artwork, for up to $100,000 of damage. It is contents coverage. Most NFIP policies take effect 30 days after you buy , so a policy bought today will not cover a flood this week. To buy: call the NFIP at (877) 336-2627 , go to FloodSmart.gov , or ask any licensed insurance agent. Every NFIP insurer offers the same rates. Federal flood insurance (NFIP) is sold only in communities that take part in the NFIP; in a community that has not joined, or once a suspension takes effect, no one can buy an NFIP policy. What it won't pay: The NFIP's standard policy for homes (the Dwelling Form) does not pay for a hotel or other living costs while you are out of the home, or for belongings outside the building, and it limits what it pays for in a basement. Belongings are paid at actual cash value (their used value), not the cost of new ones. The policy generally counts a flood only when water covers two or more acres of normally dry land or two or more properties, one of them yours. In Texas, a landlord must give a tenant a written notice saying whether the landlord is aware the home is in a 100-year floodplain, and, if the landlord knows flooding damaged it at least once in the five years before the lease, a notice saying whether the landlord is aware it flooded in that time. Some leases are exempt, including leases shorter than 30 days (Texas Property Code §92.0135). What do renters pay in each state?
