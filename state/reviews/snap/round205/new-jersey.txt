Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. New Jersey's environmental agency (NJDEP) offers New Jersey's Flood Indicator Tool , a screening tool that shows indicators of potential flood risk on or near a property, among them FEMA flood zones and State Flood Hazard Areas. NJDEP says it does not calculate actual risk, and that the absence of indicators does not mean a property is free from flood risk. 85.0% of New Jersey's NFIP policy units are in high-risk zones. That is a share of insured policy units, not of all buildings, and not an answer about your own home. Of the NFIP claim dollars paid here (1978–2026), 8.1% went to claims rated outside high-risk zones A and V. That uses the zone on each claim, not today's map, and leaves out claims with no zone. How this is counted Would a policy here pay to rebuild my home? Not in full for at least 38.1% of the 44,078 single-family policies here that report a rebuilding cost: FEMA's records put it above the NFIP's $250,000 building limit. Rebuilding cost above the building limit (of 44,078 policies reporting one) 38.1% Single-family policies insured to the building maximum (all of them, not only those above) 79.1% Damage was more than the coverage (of 163,133 claims paid with a damage figure) 2.5% The two shares have different bases: insured-to-the-maximum counts every single-family policy with the full building limit; the rebuilding-cost share counts only the policies that report a rebuilding cost. Homes insured for less than their own rebuilding cost can also fall short. How this is counted The NFIP pays the cost to rebuild (not a depreciated value) only for a single-family home that is your principal residence and is insured for at least 80% of its full rebuilding cost, or for the $250,000 maximum.
