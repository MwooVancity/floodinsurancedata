If you ask FEMA to review your property's zone , this is what its rules compare: FEMA's rules allow that only a portion of a property may be in a high-risk zone: when FEMA reviews a property's zone, it compares either the ground of an entire legally defined parcel of land or the ground next to a structure with the elevation of the base flood (44 CFR §70.5, §70.9). What the zones mean A, AE, A1–A30: high-risk. AO, AH: high-risk, shallow flooding. A99: high-risk, behind a flood barrier FEMA treats as complete. V, VE, V1–V30: coastal high-risk, flooded by tides with fast-moving water. B and shaded X: moderate flood hazard. C and unshaded X: minimal flood hazard. D: flood hazard possible but not determined. Zone codes from 44 CFR §64.3; the shaded and unshaded X labels from FEMA's flood zone glossary. What each zone costs, and what NFIP claims there paid: Zone AE Zone X Zone VE Zone A Zone AO Zone AH Zone D Must I buy flood insurance in my zone? Federal law requires it only in a high-risk zone (A or V) with a federally regulated or backed mortgage, or federal help to buy or build (42 U.S.C. §4012a). Outside those zones, a lender may still require it. If the zone looks wrong An owner or renter who believes the property was put in a high-risk A or V zone by mistake, because of how the zone line was drawn against streets or other features, can send FEMA technical information and ask for a Letter of Map Amendment, which identifies the property to be excluded from the zone (44 CFR part 70). FEMA charges no review or processing fee for a Letter of Map Amendment request (44 CFR §72.5). FEMA's Online LOMC site lets home owners or their representatives ask FEMA for a Letter of Map Change. If you think a lender's high-risk-zone finding is wrong, you and the lender together can ask FEMA to review it within 45 days of the lender's notice; a fee applies (44 CFR §65.17). The LOMA and LOMR guide shows how FEMA ruled on past requests. Is my map about to change?
