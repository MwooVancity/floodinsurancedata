Median premium charged (before fees) $804 Median full-risk premium $807 Pay below full-risk 33.9% of 171 New York: 46.1% Yearly cap: Except for the Emergency Program discount, statutory discounts gradually phase out through an annual increase cap discount at each renewal date until the policy reaches its full-risk premium. By law, most premium rates may not rise more than 18% a year; the rate for some property, including homes that are not a primary residence and business property, rises 25% a year until that group's average rate reaches the level the law sets (42 U.S.C. §4015(e)). FloodSmart puts the limit at 18 to 25% a year for most policyholders. It usually does not apply to new policies, raising your coverage or lowering your deductible can go past it, and fees and surcharges are added on top. Source: FEMA OpenFEMA — NFIP Policies. Relatively Moderate for coastal flooding and Very High for inland (river) flooding, by FEMA's county rating. That compares this county with others; it is not a forecast for your address. Coastal flood risk rating (FEMA) Relatively Moderate Inland flood risk rating (FEMA) Very High FEMA's rating weighs modeled loss with social vulnerability and community resilience; it is not a prediction for your home, and it can differ from the loss percentile below. Coastal: FEMA models roughly $1.2 million of flood loss a year (buildings, people and farmland valued in dollars, insured or not; a model, not insurance payouts); a separate FEMA measure, loss per dollar of building value: coastal flood loss here is higher than in about 48% of U.S. counties. Inland: FEMA models roughly $187.0 million of flood loss a year (buildings, people and farmland valued in dollars, insured or not; a model, not insurance payouts); a separate FEMA measure, loss per dollar of building value: inland flood loss here is higher than in about 27% of U.S. counties.
