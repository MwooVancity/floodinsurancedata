## Findings

1. MAJOR (label vs data). The denominator needs damage > $0 and coverage > $0, but every label on the page drops these. Methods: "divided by all single-family primary-home claims with a building payment in the county". Unit text used in the abstract, Results, table and Reproduce: "single-family primary-home claims with a building payment". Reproduce: "keep the single-family primary-home claims with a building payment for losses 2016-2025". Only the Data "Base:" sentence is complete. Claims with a payment but no recorded damage or no coverage are not in the n, so "of 273,450 claims with a building payment" overstates the base. Fix: put the full base in the unit string (e.g. "single-family primary-home claims with a building payment, recorded building damage and building coverage all above $0") or move the three conditions into the Methods and Reproduce sentences; state how many paid SF claims the extra conditions remove (count in/out).

2. MAJOR (label vs data). "Single-family" is never defined on the page. The code uses occupancyType single-family, primaryResidenceIndicator true AND condominiumCoverageTypeCode N or blank (condo units excluded). The page lists fields in Data but not condominiumCoverageTypeCode, and the Base sentence says only "single-family primary homes". A reader cannot reproduce from Methods alone. Fix: add condominiumCoverageTypeCode to the fields list and say "excluding condominium coverage types".

3. MAJOR (label implies something FEMA fields do not say). Title/H1/key "Underinsured" asserts owners were underinsured. The measure is recorded actual cash value of damage above the building coverage limit. The Limitations card itself says it "is not the owner's uninsured loss". A title that the body contradicts misleads a skimming reader and a search snippet. Fix: retitle to the measure ("Flood claims where recorded building damage passed the building coverage"), rename the URL later with a 301 if already staged; keep "underinsured" out of headings and CSV name.

4. MAJOR (gate M1.4 materiality missing). The page reports only the share of claims over coverage, no size. 4.9% could be $100 or $200,000 over. Gate requires a median gap/median excess. Fix: add to extras (median of dmg - cov over the numerator claims) and show it beside the share in Results and the CSV (with its n = numerator), or state that it was not computed and why.

5. MAJOR (gate M1.8 one event). Page text has no one-event statement. County claims cluster by flood event (Humphreys TN 54 of 103 is plausibly one 2021 event; Texas, Louisiana, West Virginia counties in the top 20 likely one storm). Wilson treats claims as independent, so the printed 90% ranges for those counties are too narrow, and the shared methods concede "claims in one county are not independent" while still printing a binomial interval per county. Fix: compute loss-year share of the numerator per county in extras; where one loss year is more than half the numerator, print "most of these claims are from one loss year (YYYY)" in the row; add one sentence that county ranges assume independent claims and are narrower than the truth for single-event counties.

6. MINOR to MAJOR if kept (selection/winner's curse). The top fifth is dominated by small-n counties: 18 of the 54 top-fifth counties have n < 150, and the headline "highest share is Humphreys 52.4% (54 of 103)" is an n=103 county ranked by point estimate. The abstract names it as the maximum. Fix: rank/band by interval (or call out in the abstract that the top counties have among the smallest n), and keep "highest share" tied to the n beside it in the same sentence. Not a BLOCKER since n and range are printed.

7. MINOR (mixed bases in one abstract sentence). "4.9% of 303,311 ... across all U.S. counties combined ... ; across the 268 counties with at least 100 each, 4.9% (90% range 3.9-5.8%, resampling counties)". Both are 4.9% on different bases; the range belongs only to the second, but the sentence reads as one figure. Fix: separate sentences; say the all-county figure has no range because it is not resampled. Also "all U.S. counties" excludes claims with blank or unmatched countyCode (same point as outside-zone round 4 m1).

8. MINOR (bootstrap). pooled_boot: B=2000, seed fixed, indexes int(0.05*B)=100 and int(0.95*B)-1=1899, i.e. the 101st and 1900th of 2000 sorted values; acceptable percentile, slightly asymmetric. Resampling only the 268 listed counties makes the range dominated by the few counties with n over 3,000 (Montgomery, Fort Bend, Harris); stating that the pooled share is claim-weighted would help. No fix required.

9. MINOR (Texas statement is a bare ratio). "10 of the 50 counties with the highest share ... are in Texas." Texas has 28 of the 268 listed counties (10.4%), so 20% of the top 50 is about double the list share, but the page gives no list-share beside it. Also invites a causal reading. Fix: add "(Texas has 28 of the 268 counties)" or delete the sentence (it answers no reader question).

10. MINOR (measure validity, say it). Damage is ACV, coverage is the policy limit; for single-family primary homes NFIP pays replacement cost up to the limit when insured to value, so ACV damage above the limit is a conservative count of claims at the limit. Also no deductible, no "coverage" at loss date vs policy at as-of. Limitations does not say damage values are recorded by the adjuster and unaudited (outliers, keying errors). Fix: one added limitation line: "damage is as recorded by FEMA; extreme values are not audited."

11. MINOR (no inflation/dollar label needed). The share compares two dollar fields of the same claim, so the gate M1.5 inflation rule does not apply; fine. The window sentence ("losses 2016-2025", ten whole years) is stated in the lede, Data, CSV: PASS.

12. MINOR (causation, risk, advice). Scan of the page text found no because/due to/driven by/significant/best/worst/safest; no recommendation; fifth explained as "a position in this list, not a grade, a risk score or a prediction": PASS. Prior work line is dated and names the search; it is thin ("WebSearch: NFIP claims damage exceeded coverage by county"), the gate asks for 3+ queries; log the other queries.
