# Data-content formats that earn news links, and how journalists find and cite data (US flood/insurance focus)

Research date: 2026-11-10. Evidence note: most "format" numbers come from agency blogs and case studies (self-reported marketing). Journalist-behaviour numbers come from vendor surveys (Muck Rack, Cision), often read through secondary summaries because the full reports are gated. Each line says which kind it is.

## Which formats earned the most referring domains (2020-2026 case studies)?

### Takeaway
No independent, methodology-published study ranks formats by referring domains. Agency evidence consistently points to original data studies (often with a map or ranking), and Fractl's own analysis says filled maps and images beat infographics, charts and video. Campaigns typically earn tens of referring domains; data assets can keep earning links for years.

### Cited Findings
- Digitaloft research (as reported by Go Fish Digital): the average digital PR campaign earns links from 42 unique domains — [Go Fish Digital](https://gofishdigital.com/blog/digital-pr-increase-online-authority/)
- Go Fish Digital: 78% of campaigns that use created or scraped data earn organic media coverage (agency claim) — [Go Fish Digital](https://gofishdigital.com/blog/digital-pr-increase-online-authority/)
- Reboot Online: 42.3% of its own 2023-24 digital PR campaigns were data-driven — [Reboot Online Digital PR Stats 2025 (PDF)](https://www.rebootonline.com/static/images/digital-pr-statistics-2025/Digital%20PR%20Stats%20HRP%20PDF%20v7.pdf)
- Fractl, 290 campaigns since 2014, 4,000+ international placements. Among its top 50 campaigns, filled maps and images performed best; bar graphs, illustrated charts and doughnut charts were moderate; traditional charts, videos and infographics "trailed significantly". The 50 campaigns with the most domestic placements were also the top international performers. The article's own advice lists infographics as a top pick, which contradicts its chart — [Fractl](https://www.frac.tl/?p=10976)
- Fractl also advises avoiding US-centric framing for international pickup, even with US data — [Fractl](https://www.frac.tl/?p=10976)
- moveBuddha (moving marketplace): about five years of regular digital PR took it from 582 referring domains (2021) to 4,322 (Dec 2025); one proprietary data study plus interactive data visuals earned 12 media placements in its first 2 months (agency case study) — [Green Flag Digital](https://greenflagdigital.com/portfolio/moving-marketplace-digital-pr-content-marketing/)
- Evergreen example: a survey page had 7 backlinks after its launch push and later reached 119+ referring domains, so data pages keep earning after the pitch window — [Go Fish Digital, evergreen campaigns](https://gofishdigital.com/blog/how-to-build-evergreen-digital-pr-campaigns-that-keep-earning-links/)
- Agencies list the dependable formats as research-backed data studies, surveys with unique findings, interactive tools/visuals and topical (reactive) content; others add rankings and quizzes — [Go Fish Digital, home & real estate guide](https://gofishdigital.com/blog/guide-to-link-building-campaigns-in-home-and-real-estate/); [Positional](https://www.positional.com/blog/building-links-with-digital-pr)
- City studies (rank every city/metro on one metric) are promoted by BuzzStream as a "tried-and-true" format because they localize into many news stories — [BuzzStream, city studies](https://www.buzzstream.com/?p=7915)
- An older BuzzStream survey of link builders: long-form, report-style content was the top choice for link campaigns. A Backlinko study found "why" and "what" posts and infographics got 25.8% more links than videos and how-to posts (older, pre-2020) — [BuzzStream](https://www.buzzstream.com/?p=6255); [Backlinko content study](https://backlinko.com/content-study)
- Flood-specific proof that a "your county" breakdown gets cited: during Hurricane Helene (2024), NBC News, Reuters, the Washington Post and MoneyGeek all published county-level flood-insurance take-up figures from NFIP policy data (NBC: ~2% of residences in the 100 hardest-hit counties; Reuters: 0.5% in western hardest-hit counties vs 43% of single-family homes in Dare County; MoneyGeek: ~6,950 policies, ~1.5% penetration across 23 western NC counties). Mitchell County NC: 31 policies for 8,600+ housing units (FEMA figure). The outlets used different denominators, so the numbers are not comparable — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/data-graphics/map-flood-insurance-coverage-counties-hit-hurricane-helene-rcna173619); [Anchorage Daily News / AP-style syndication](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2024/10/03/nearly-all-homes-in-counties-hardest-hit-by-helene-lack-national-flood-insurance/); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2024/10/07/796083.htm); [MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/homeowners/flood-insurance-coverage-by-state/); [Fox 29](https://www.fox29.com/news/hurricane-helene-flood-insurance-fema-nfip)

### Inferences
- For a flood site, the format with direct evidence is a **county-level filled map/table of one federal figure** (NFIP take-up, claims paid, policies dropped), because that is exactly what national outlets built during Helene. A site that already has that per county, dated and sourced, is the thing a reporter needs on deadline.
- "Your county" lookups and rankings work because each county is a separate local story; a single national ranking localizes into ~50 state stories and hundreds of county ones.
- Shareable images matter more than embeddable infographics, per Fractl's top-50 split.
- Downloadable CSVs have no direct link-count evidence in the case studies, but they are a hard entry criterion for data newsletters (see Data Is Plural below).

### Gaps
- No independent study (Moz, Search Engine Land, Siege Media) with published sample and method ranking formats by referring domains, 2020-2026, was found. Treat all format numbers as agency claims.
- No numbers found on annual indexes vs one-off studies, or on interactive maps vs static maps specifically for links.
- MoneyGeek/NBC Helene pieces: no data found on how many referring domains they earned.

## Newsjacking: how data sites get cited during hurricanes and floods

### Takeaway
Reactive PR guidance says the window is hours (commonly "2-6 hours", some say minutes, at most ~24 hours). In Helene the cited angle was "how few homes in the hit counties had flood insurance", built from NFIP policy data within about a week of landfall. Accuracy is stressed more in disaster coverage than in normal newsjacking.

### Cited Findings
- Optimal window cited as the first 2-6 hours after a story breaks; others say minutes; another says as short as 24 hours (agency guidance, not research) — [Anicca](https://anicca.co.uk/newsjacking-reactive-pr/); [BuzzStream, newsjacking 2025](https://buzzstream.com/?p=8375); [Shno newsjacking stats](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/newsjacking-statistics)
- Reactive PR = pitching quotes, data or a new story that ties into a trending event; one provider targets "expert quote with supporting data within two hours" — [BuzzStream, reactive PR](https://www.buzzstream.com/?p=9093); [Starkrank reactive PR](https://starkrank.pages.dev/services/reactive-pr)
- Guides warn to confirm accuracy and timeliness of any cited data in reactive pieces — [Evoluted](https://evoluted.net/blog/marketing/a-guide-to-successful-newsjacking-top-tips-from-the-best-in-digital-pr); [Everything PR five-question test](https://everything-pr.com/newsjacking-rules-five-question-test)
- Helene landfall was 26 Sept 2024; NBC, Reuters and Washington Post county flood-insurance analyses ran around 3-7 Oct 2024 (dates from URLs/publication: ADN 2024/10/03, Insurance Journal 2024/10/07) — [ADN](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2024/10/03/nearly-all-homes-in-counties-hardest-hit-by-helene-lack-national-flood-insurance/); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2024/10/07/796083.htm); [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2024/10/03/267204.htm)
- NBC combined Census, PowerOutage.us and NFIP policy data (via Neptune Flood, a private insurer) — showing that a third party's data was cited by a national outlet during the disaster — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/data-graphics/map-flood-insurance-coverage-counties-hit-hurricane-helene-rcna173619)
- Reporters on time pressure: Google News Lab found 49% of data stories are made in a day or less — [Google blog, State of data journalism 2017](https://www.blog.google/outreach-initiatives/google-news-initiative/data-journalism-2017/)

### Inferences
- The flood questions reporters asked in Helene coverage map to: "what share of homes here have flood insurance", "how many policies were dropped", "what do claims pay here", "is this county in the NFIP / CRS". A page that answers these per county before the storm (already indexed) is findable at the 2-6 hour mark; a page built after landfall is usually too late.
- The citation went to whoever held county-level numbers ready; Neptune Flood got named by NBC. Being the pre-built, dated, sourced county page is the free version of that.

### Gaps
- No survey of what local TV/newspaper reporters type into Google during a hurricane. Inference only.
- No hurricane-specific newsjacking case study with link counts was found.

## What journalists need before citing a source; does an anonymous site get cited?

### Takeaway
Journalists ask for relevance, original data, images and access to experts, and say accuracy/verification is a top challenge. Editors say they delete rankings without a clear methodology. No survey measures anonymous sites directly; the evidence points to methodology + verifiable public source as the substitute for a named author, and a named contact being normal in pitches.

### Cited Findings
- Cision 2025 State of the Media (3,000+ journalists, 19 markets): 72% rate press releases most useful; 79% rely on them for story ideas; 57% want exclusives; 55% want original research; 70% want images with a pitch; 54% say ideal pitch includes "compelling data or statistics"; 49% want a unique angle — [Cision UK, PR lessons from State of the Media](https://www.cision.co.uk/resources/articles/pr-lessons-state-of-the-media/); [Cision press release](https://www.cision.com/about/press-releases/2025-press-releases/cisions-2025-state-of-the-media-report-reveals-a-tipping-point-for-trust-technology-and-pr-journalist-partnerships-302448410/)
- Cision 2026 State of the Media (1,800+ reporters): 50% say striving for accuracy and fighting misinformation is a top challenge (40% the year before); 66% use PR-provided content for story ideas; 97% prefer email; most say 25% or fewer pitches are relevant; 82% reject pitches mainly for audience/coverage misfit; journalists want data, embargoes and expert access — [PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/data-experts-and-early-access-what-journalists-want-in-2026/)
- Muck Rack State of Journalism 2025 (1,500+ journalists, April 2025, mostly North America), via secondary summaries: 69% prefer pitches under 200 words; 78% want pitches before noon; 86% cite irrelevance as top reason to delete; 62% prefer 1:1 email; journalists want verified sources, third-party data and credible experts — [PR News](https://www.prnewsonline.com/the-state-of-journalism-in-2025-insights-to-optimize-your-media-relations/); [Michael Smart PR](https://michaelsmartpr.com/what-1500-journalists-just-told-us-about-pr/); [Muck Rack webinar FAQ](https://muckrack.com/blog/2025/07/09/state-of-journalism-2025-webinar-faqs)
- Muck Rack 2026: 47% say pitches are seldom or never relevant — [University of Minnesota summary](https://umarcomm.umn.edu/blog/2026/04/13/pitching-journalists-muck-racks-state-journalism-2026) (page returned 403 to fetch; figure from search snippet)
- Times of San Diego editor: first check on any ranking is the methodology; if it doesn't clearly say how the ranking was done, the email is deleted — [Times of San Diego](https://timesofsandiego.com/?p=250815)
- 2021 survey of 1,026 journalists: 53% find information from PR pros inaccurate — [GRC Survey 2022 (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/60beeb013f3791685653cab1/t/638fae0a272c80093e1c3f52/1670360590433/GRC_Survey-2022.pdf)
- US daily-newspaper reporters routinely use local/state/federal government websites for data and rate state data most accurate — [Reynolds Journalism Institute / Missouri Press](https://mopress.com/?p=843)
- Data newsletter entry criteria (Data Is Plural): free and public with no registration, downloadable in bulk (CSV/JSON preferred), well documented (method, variables, known issues), accurate, clear terms of use/licence, a unique linkable URL, original, fresh, detailed — [Data Is Plural criteria](https://www.data-is-plural.com/faq/criteria/)

### Inferences
- What substitutes for a named author on an anonymous site: a methodology page, the federal source and as-of date on every figure, a CSV download per figure, a stable URL per county, and a clear licence/attribution line. Each of these appears in a journalist or curator requirement above.
- A contact route (email address, even role-based) is implied by 97% email preference; the surveys don't say it must be a named person.
- Anonymous sites are more exposed to the "who is behind this?" check because the vendor surveys show accuracy is the rising concern.

### Gaps
- No survey asks whether journalists cite anonymous websites. Not found in Muck Rack, Cision, or trade press.
- No survey numbers on embeddable charts or on local breakdowns specifically.

## How reporters find data, and which channels are free

### Takeaway
Google (first page) and government websites remain the default discovery path; PR email is the top inspiration source. Journalist-request services changed: Connectively closed Dec 2024, Featured relaunched HARO in April 2025; Source of Sources is free. Data Is Plural takes email submissions free.

### Cited Findings
- Dutch newsroom experiment (73 journalists): all used Google and mostly stayed on the first results page; a German study found Google used for 90.4% of searches (older studies) — [Kemman et al. 2013 (Scribd)](https://es.scribd.com/document/366985094/Kemman-et-al-2013-Dutch-Journalism-in-the-Digital-Age-pdf); [UTS chapter](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/31976/4/Information%20sources%20and%20data%20discovery_Challenge%20and%20change_Chapter%208.pdf)
- 95% of business journalists use search engines (older survey) — [PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/?p=278605)
- More than half of reporters polled had spiked stories because of what online search showed about a company — [Lumentus / PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/your-online-corporate-reputation-could-kill-your-next-earned-media-opportunity-lumentus-journalist-survey-300652036.html)
- Cision 2025: 96% of journalists use social media for work — [PR Newswire media blog](https://mediablog.prnewswire.com/2025/05/14/2025-state-of-the-media-takeaways)
- HARO: Connectively (Cision's HARO rebrand) shut down 9 Dec 2024; Featured.com bought HARO, announced 15 Apr 2025, emails resumed 22 Apr 2025. Sources disagree whether the relaunch is free, paid or ad-supported — [BuzzStream, HARO is back](https://www.buzzstream.com/?p=10080); [Octiv Digital](https://www.octivdigital.com/ideas-and-advice/featured-acquires-haro-rolls-it-back-to-version-users-love/); [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/featured-acquires-haro); [That Agency](https://thatagency.com/blog/tips-for-using-connectively-to-build-high-quality-backlinks/)
- Connectively reportedly relaunched under Featured in 2026 with 100,000+ users (single source) — [TechIntelPro](https://techintelpro.com/news/marketing/content-marketing/connectively-surpasses-100000-users-after-featured-revival)
- Qwoted described as closest to original HARO with verified journalists; free tier unconfirmed; Source of Sources (Peter Shankman) described as free — [blckalpaca HARO alternatives 2026](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/seo-geo/off-page-seo-link-building/haro-alternatives-2026-featured-qwoted-and-source-of-sources); [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/haro-alternatives-2026)
- Data Is Plural: weekly dataset newsletter by Jeremy Singer-Vine, 400 editions Oct 2015-Aug 2025; suggestions by email (jsvine@gmail.com), free — [Data Is Plural](https://data-is-plural.com/); [criteria](https://www.data-is-plural.com/faq/criteria/); [Buttondown profile](https://buttondown.com/blog/data-is-plural)
- Data journalists' resource lists (e.g. VOA "datasets for journalists") are another discovery path — [VOA datasets for journalists](https://voanews.github.io/datasets-for-journalists)

### Inferences
- Free channels ranked by evidence: (1) ranking on Google's first page for "<county> flood insurance policies" style queries, (2) direct 1:1 email pitch under 200 words before noon, (3) Data Is Plural submission once a bulk CSV exists, (4) Source of Sources (free) and possibly HARO/Qwoted free tiers.
- Note for an anonymous site: HARO/Qwoted-style services generally expect a named expert source to quote, which an anonymous site can't supply; data-only answers fit Data Is Plural and direct pitches better.

### Gaps
- No evidence found on r/dataisbeautiful as a journalist discovery channel (no searches returned it with data).
- Current pricing of HARO (Featured) and Qwoted free tiers unconfirmed; check their sites.

## Shock-value stats without losing trust; backlash examples

### Takeaway
"Worst city" rankings and precise-looking risk scores get pushback when the method is vague, data old, or small denominators inflate rates. The closest flood example is First Street: lawsuits, a state AG suit, and Zillow/MLS pulling its scores over accuracy at the property level. Google treats links obtained through link schemes as spam, which rules out paid/swapped links.

### Cited Findings
- South Fulton (Georgia) cities pushed back on a "worst cities" ranking: old data, and per-100,000 crime rates are unfair for small cities — [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/local-govt--politics/metro-atlanta-cities-push-back-after-being-named-worst-cities/8grTxIOfSK5Ji7arnRdr6K)
- WalletHub's "second-worst-run city" ranking of San Francisco drew City Hall pushback; commentators call many startup-site best/worst lists methodologically unserious — [SFist](https://sfist.com/2022/06/30/after-wallethub-names-sf-second-worst-run-city-in-america-city-hall-officials-are-none-too-pleased); [Metro Times, Detroit](https://www.metrotimes.com/news/detroit-once-again-ranked-as-the-worst-big-city-to-live-in-heres-why-were-calling-bull-shit-14068275/); [Greene Barrett](https://www.greenebarrett.com/post/why-many-state-and-city-rankings-defy-reality)
- FTN News described misleading "research" used as backlink bait (a London agency's European culture ranking missed Florence because a source listed it as "Firenze") — [FTN News](https://ftnnews.com/?p=52461)
- Amazon "cleanest cities" list called inaccurate (2016) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/amazons-10-cleanest-cities-data-inaccurate)
- First Street (flood risk scores): Florida AG sued alleging misleading scores and "deceptive" peer-review claims; homeowner complaints to the NC AG; a New York couple sued First Street and Zillow; California Regional MLS pushed platforms to stop showing the data; Zillow moved scores off listings to external links in late 2025; academics and insurers say pinpoint precision can mislead; a UC Irvine study found national flood models may be inaccurate locally — [ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/florida-ag-sues-climate-analytics-provider-first-street-over-home-risk-scor/873473/); [NewsChannel 9](https://newschannel9.com/news/local/western-north-carolina-wnc-property-homeowners-question-accuracy-climate-risk-ratings-impacting-home-sales-investigation-data-first-street-tech-asheville-hendersonville-realtor-flood-helene); [Local 12](https://local12.com/news/nation-world/climate-risk-scores-on-home-listings-draw-scrutiny-from-conservative-groups-fema-property-cosumers); [Smart Cities Dive](https://www.smartcitiesdive.com/news/flood-climate-risk-data-models-accurate-first-street-UC-irvine/723496/)
- First Street's earlier viral stat ("FEMA massively undercounting flood risk", ~2x properties at 100-year risk vs FEMA maps) was widely covered — [Gizmodo 2020](https://gizmodo.com/report-finds-fema-is-massively-undercounting-flood-risk-1844202861); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/femas-flood-maps-are-basically-lies-2000642351)
- Google spam policies: links bought or exchanged to manipulate rankings ("link schemes", including excessive link exchanges and paid links that pass ranking credit) are spam — [Google Search spam policies, link spam](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam) (well-known policy page; not re-fetched in this session)

### Inferences
- Safe "shock" stats are ones that are true at the unit shown, with the base stated: e.g. "31 NFIP policies for 8,600+ housing units in Mitchell County (FEMA)". The backlash cases came from modelled/property-level precision, opaque weighting, old data and small-denominator rates.
- Avoid composite "worst county" scores; rank on a single published federal figure with the denominator and as-of date shown, and flag small counts.

### Gaps
- No documented case found of a small anonymous data site specifically called out for a misleading flood stat.
- No measurement of whether corrections/backlash cost a site its existing links.
