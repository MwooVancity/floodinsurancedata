# US flood-insurance and flood-data stories that spread, 2018-2026

Evidence key: **[READ]** = page fetched and read directly this session. **[SUMMARY]** = seen only in search-result summaries or snippets; the URL is real, but the figure was not checked on the page itself. Only one page was read directly (NBC). The NRDC press release returned 403. Treat every [SUMMARY] figure as a lead. Recheck it before it goes on a page.

## Which stories got picked up widely?

### Takeaway
The stories that spread had three things in common. They put one surprising number against an official baseline ("FEMA says X, the real number is Y"), they had a local cut so every outlet could localise it (county, ZIP, metro), and they came out within days of a disaster news peg (Harvey, Helene, the Washington State atmospheric river). Investigative series spread less than single stats with a local lookup.

### Cited Findings (examples)

By 2050, 16.2 million. Washington, DC deviation +438%. Format: national report + free address lookup (floodfactor.com) + local rankings. Spread: CBS News, Fox News, USA Today, Claims Journal, GovExec/Route Fifty ("Millions of Homeowners Who Need Flood Insurance Don't Know It—Thanks to FEMA"), Dayton Daily News and Columbus local cuts, GovTech, Security Magazine, PreventionWeb. FEMA gave a public response. Why it spread: an authority-contradiction headline, a local cut for every metro, a free tool readers could try on their own address, and FEMA's reply that kept the story going. ### Inferences
- Common formula: public federal dataset (NFIP policies, FEMA ZIP table, repetitive-loss list) / a denominator (housing units, at-risk homes) = one share, published by county within 72 hours of a disaster.
- The most-linked format is "official number vs real number" (First Street vs FEMA maps, inland vs coastal coverage). The second is the vivid outlier (the house flooded 40 times).
- A lookup tool (Flood Factor, Zillow listing scores, FEMA ZIP table) produces a long tail of local stories. One-off reports get a single news cycle.

### Gaps
- No ProPublica, NYT Upshot or Bloomberg CityLab NFIP piece was confirmed in these searches (I searched with limited calls). Their reach is unverified.
- No link counts (referring domains) were found for any example. That would need a backlink tool (Ahrefs/Moz), which was not used.

## Which single stats got repeated most?

### Takeaway
The most repeated stats were uninsured shares near zero ("under 1%", "0.8%", "about 2%", "80% uninsured"), the FEMA-undercount ratio (14.6M vs 8.7M), the 40-times-flooded house, and Risk Rating 2.0's "77% will pay more".

### Inferences
- Stats spread when they need no table: a share under 1%, a "times" count, or a dollar total. Each is checked against the reader's prior belief ("my homeowners policy covers floods").
- Several repeated stats have shaky or conflicting sources (the Harvey 80%, Pew's 1% vs R Street's 2%). A site with exact, sourced, dated county figures can be the citable correction.

### Gaps
- I found no frequency count (how many articles carry each stat). The ranking above is qualitative.

## Did small or new sites get news links from data?

### Takeaway
Yes, but mostly mid-sized specialists, not tiny newcomers. First Street was a young nonprofit (founded around 2018) when its 2020 launch beat FEMA in the headlines. Neptune Flood (private insurer) gets steady trade-press and some national pickup (its data fed NBC's Helene map) from state reports built on public NFIP data. Asheville Watchdog, a small local nonprofit, got regional republication but no confirmed national credit.

### Cited Findings
- First Street, a young nonprofit, got national TV and wire coverage with its first release in 2020 — [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/15-million-homes-at-risk-of-flooding-new-data-from-first-street-foundation/)
- NBC's Helene map used "NFIP policy data collected by Neptune Flood" — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/data-graphics/map-flood-insurance-coverage-counties-hit-hurricane-helene-rcna173619) [READ]
- Neptune reports picked up by Insurance Journal, Claims Journal, Carrier Management, PC360, Insurance Business — [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2025/08/07/834908.htm)
- Asheville Watchdog's Buncombe analysis was republished by Mountain Xpress, asheville.com and Good Men Project. No national credit was found — [Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/?p=852468)
- Personal-finance comparison sites (Policygenius, ValuePenguin) published RR2.0 ZIP/city cuts that were cited in insurance coverage — [Policygenius](https://policygenius.com/homeowners-insurance/risk-rating-flood-insurance-increase); [ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/new-risk-rating-flood-insurance-rate-increases)
- UC Davis California WaterBlog's "first look" at RR2.0 was reposted by PreventionWeb — [California WaterBlog](https://californiawaterblog.com/2021/09/19/risk-rating-2-0-a-first-look-at-femas-new-flood-insurance-system/embed/)

### Inferences
- What got a small or specialist site cited: (a) it was first with a county-level number after a disaster, (b) it published the data so journalists could reuse it (NBC used Neptune's collected NFIP data), and (c) it pitched trade press, which national reporters read.
- For floodinsurancedata.com: county NFIP policies per housing unit, the change over 10 years, and repetitive-loss counts, all ready on day 1 of a named flood, are the same shape as items 3-5 and 11.

### Gaps
- I found no confirmed case of a brand-new anonymous site earning a national news link from flood data. Evidence for "small site" wins is limited to nonprofits, insurers and local newsrooms.
- Referring-domain counts for Neptune, First Street and Asheville Watchdog pages were not checked.
