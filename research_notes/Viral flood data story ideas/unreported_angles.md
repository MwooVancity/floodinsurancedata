# Unreported or under-reported angles in public FEMA/NOAA flood data (county / ZIP / zone aggregates)

Research date: 2026-11-10. Planning notes only; no figure below has been computed from raw data. Every number is quoted from the linked source. "Novelty" means how much coverage I found in about 15 searches and fetches. It is not proof that nobody has published the angle.

## Q1. Which "shock but strictly true" angles exist, and has each been covered?

### Takeaway
The national versions of almost every obvious angle are already covered: the share of claims outside high-risk zones, the gap between disaster grants and NFIP payouts, repetitive-loss concentration, old maps, the NFIP debt, the Helene coverage gap and the coastal/inland cross-subsidy. The open space is the **county- and ZIP-level versions**, plus a few field combinations I found no coverage of: rated zone vs current zone, reasons claims were not paid, the gap between damage and payout, and second-home share.

### Cited Findings

**A. Covered nationally or by state; county level is the open space**

1. **Claims outside high-risk zones.** FEMA: "almost one-third" of NFIP claims from 2014 to 2024 came from outside high-risk areas ([FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/node/4024)). CRS gives about 20% ([CRS IN10890](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20180910_IN10890_f6d16ecbee2f25ece61efb428997c48ce69e1136.html)). Neptune gives 45% ([InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/flood-risk-doesnt-stop-at-the-100-year-floodplain-line-data-shows)). A state list puts DC at 85.6%, Utah at 81.3% and Wyoming at 61.6% (seen in search snippets only, via the same coverage cluster). It is unverified. A Texas Tech 2026 poster gives 26.8% outside the SFHA, analysed by tract ([TTU CECON 2026](https://www.depts.ttu.edu/cecreh/documentation/cecon-2026-posters/CECON2026_POSTER_Jesse.pdf)). **I found no county-by-county ranking.** Novelty: medium (county level).
2. **Policy drop after Risk Rating 2.0.** A peer-reviewed estimate puts the fall at 11-39% for new policies and 5-13% for existing ones, with larger effects in lower-income ZIPs ([Journal of Catastrophe Research](https://journalofcrr.com/research/03-07-gourevitch-et-al/)). FEMA said it "would not be accurate" to tie the loss of 500,000 policies directly to RR2.0 ([InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/femas-risk-rating-2-0-is-reshaping-flood-insurance-leaving-many-households-financially-vulnerable-to-growing-flood-risk)). Moody's: New Jersey policies fell 18.4% from 2012 to Aug 2026 ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/flood-awareness-rises-but-coverage-slips-in-sandyhit-states-595663.aspx)). Novelty: low-medium. A plain county list of "policies in force, 2021 vs latest" was not found.
3. **Disaster grants vs NFIP payout per household.** CRS: the average NFIP payout in 2016-2023 was $57,410, against an average FEMA IHP grant of $3,014 ([CRS R48310](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2024-11-05_R48310_34c9ed107b6f9cf679f678a9f315dc875d10324b.html), as cited in search results). FEMA outreach material gives about $5,000 vs about $69,000 ([FloodSmart agents](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/articles/what-your-clients-need-know-about-disaster-assistance-and-flood-insurance)). Widely covered nationally. **A per-county, per-disaster version (HA Owners vs NFIP claims for the same event) was not found.** Novelty: medium.
4. **Flood disasters with almost no policies.** Helene: 0.5% of homes in the hardest-hit western NC counties had coverage (Reuters, via [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2024/10/03/267204.htm)). Others give about 1% ([WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/2025-10-09/the-nations-largest-flood-insurance-program-expired-what-does-it-mean-for-wnc)) and 5.2% ([NC Newsline](https://ncnewsline.com/2025/04/22/few-were-insured-against-helenes-floods-those-that-were-faced-long-claims-frustrating-hurdles/)). Mitchell County had 31 policies for more than 8,600 housing units ([Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/real-estate/national-news/2024/10/04/thousands-of-uninsured-homes-were-in-hurricane-helene/)). Heavily covered for Helene. **A national ranking (number of flood declarations vs policies per housing unit, all counties) was not found.** Novelty: medium.
5. **Repetitive-loss concentration.** GAO: unmitigated repetitive-loss properties are about 2.5% of policies but received 48% of claim dollars ([GAO-26-109045](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109045)). In 2004 the figures were about 1% and 38% ([GAO-04-401T](https://gao.gov/assets/a110627.html)). From 2009 to 2018 the number of RL properties grew by about 65,000 while fewer than 20,000 were mitigated ([GAO-20-509](https://gao.gov/products/gao-20-509); press summary). Widely covered nationally. A county ranking from the Multiple Loss aggregates is less covered. Novelty: low-medium.
6. **Oldest effective flood maps.** CBO 2017: half of the 166 highest-risk counties had maps more than 5 years old, and 42 had maps more than 10 years old. In 2024, "more than 3,000 counties" had maps more than 15 years old ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2024/10/09/796507.htm)). California FIRMs rest on studies with an average date of 1993 ([California Water Blog](https://californiawaterblog.com/2019/06/16/flood-mapping-in-california-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly/)). An independent, non-peer-reviewed 2026 project ranks about 1,099 counties ([dd4all](https://dd4all.vercel.app/projects/flood-map-reality-gap)). Novelty: low-medium. Caveat: the effective date is not the date of the study under the map.
7. **Who pays in vs who gets paid ("subsidised by others").** Wharton, 1978-2008: Floridians paid $3.60 in premiums per $1 of claims, Colorado $15.1, New Mexico $10.4. Mississippi received about $5 per premium dollar and Louisiana about $3.33 ([Wharton](https://esg.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/WRCib2011b-nfip-who-pays.pdf)). CBO 2017 found a cross-subsidy from inland to coastal counties ([Insurance Journal on CBO](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2017/09/18/464180.htm)). Neptune (2026): Florida holds 47.5% of remaining subsidy dollars, and Plaquemines Parish has an 88.0% subsidised share (search snippet; the [page](https://neptuneflood.com/?p=8505) returned 403 and could not be read). GAO-09-12 has a state appendix for 1978-2007 ([GAO-09-12](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-09-12.pdf)). **I found no post-2009 county version from OpenFEMA.** Novelty: medium. Misleading risk: high (see Q3).
8. **Claim concentration.** An E&E analysis found that 8 counties got half of 2020 flood claims ([E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/analysis-8-counties-got-half-of-2020-flood-claims/)). Covered.
9. **NFIP debt.** $22.525B after a $2B draw on 2025-02-10, with about $7.9B of borrowing authority left. The program, meaning current and future policyholders, owes it ([FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/print/pdf/node/692081); [CRS IN10784](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN10784.html)). Widely covered. It is national context only and cannot be computed by county.
10. **Community Rating System discounts.** CRS communities are more populous, wealthier and more educated ([Gourevitch & Pinter](https://californiawaterblog.com/2022/02/20/femas-community-rating-system-worth-the-effort/)). Discounts are cross-subsidised by non-CRS communities, but the effect is "not economically large", and risk, not income, is the strongest predictor ([Brent et al. 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/agrerw/v53y2024i1p87-118_5.html)). Covered academically; little consumer coverage. Novelty: medium.
11. **Water depth.** A Nature Communications study of about 2.09M claims (1972-2014) found that losses do not rise steadily with depth. About 5% of records appear to be in the wrong unit ([Nature Comms 2020](https://www.Nature.Com/articles/s41467-020-15264-2)). Covered academically.

**B. No coverage found (candidate "information gain" angles)**

12. **The map changed under the claim:** the share of claims whose `ratedFloodZone` is X/B/C (low or moderate risk) but whose `floodZoneCurrent` is A or V, by county. Both fields exist ([OpenFEMA Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)). No coverage found.
13. **Why claims were not paid,** by county: shares of closed-without-payment claims for 01 below deductible, 02 seepage, 06 not an actual flood and 15 damage before the policy started (`nonPaymentReasonBuilding`, [Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)). No coverage found. Strong reader value ("what floods pay here").
14. **Damage vs payout gap:** sum of `buildingDamageAmount` minus sum of `amountPaidOnBuildingClaim` by county or event. Both fields exist ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)). No coverage found.
15. **Second homes:** share of policies or claims with `primaryResidenceIndicator` = false, by county. The field definition is in [Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). I did not search for coverage of this.
16. **Cause mix:** share of claims from code 4, rainfall or snowmelt accumulation, against code 2 (river) and code 1 (tidal), by county. This shows "it wasn't a river" flooding inland ([Claims v3 cause codes](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)). No coverage found.

### Inferences
- Before any national-level shock line is used, the strongest unclaimed angles for this site are 12, 13, 14 and 16. They come straight from claims fields, need no outside denominator, and answer reader questions the site already lists ("what floods pay here", "your exact zone").
- Angles 3 and 4 at county level need a denominator that comes from outside FEMA claims: Census housing units, or HA Owners counts. The site's rules list Census take-up as cut, so 4 must use FEMA's own penetration data, which was restored on 11-08.
- Angle 9 (the debt) is context, not a county finding.

### Gaps
- No verified primary source for the state shares outside the SFHA (DC 85.6%, Utah 81.3%, Wyoming 61.6%). They appeared only in a snippet.
- The Neptune 2026 subsidy page returned 403. Its county figures are unverified.
- I did not check whether any outlet publishes county "not-paid reason" shares or rated-vs-current zone mismatch. "No coverage found" rests on a small number of searches.

## Q2. Which exact fields compute each angle?

### Takeaway
Every angle maps to named OpenFEMA fields. The policy angles need `policyCount` summed, and the claims angles need the claims `countyCode`, which comes with a known caveat.

### Cited Findings
- Claims v3 fields: `countyCode`, `reportedZipCode`, `ratedFloodZone`, `floodZoneCurrent`, `waterDepth`, `causeOfDamage`, `buildingDamageAmount`, `amountPaidOnBuildingClaim`, `nonPaymentReasonBuilding`, `primaryResidenceIndicator`, `rateMethod`, `eventDesignationNumber` (from June 2020), `ficoNumber` (before then), `floodEvent`, `yearOfLoss`, `dateOfLoss`. There are 2,725,989 records, refreshed monthly; the last refresh was 2026-09-09 ([OpenFEMA Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).
- Claims v2 is deprecated, frozen at 2026-06-01, with removal due by 2026-10-15 ([OpenFEMA Claims v2](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/fima-nfip-redacted-claims-v2)).
- `rateMethod` "RatingEngine" marks FEMA risk-rated (RR2.0) policies, `R` marks newly mapped into the SFHA, and `7`/`P`/`Q` mark Preferred Risk ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).
- The HA Owners dataset is aggregated by state, county and ZIP, with inspected-damage bands and approved amounts ([data.gov catalog](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/housing-assistance-program-data-owners-nemis)).

### Inferences
- Angle 1/12: `ratedFloodZone` and `floodZoneCurrent` grouped by `countyCode`. Exclude blank zones and print the excluded count.
- Angle 2: Policies v3 `policyCount` summed, by `countyCode` and policy year (for example as of each Oct 1). Never count rows.
- Angle 3: HA Owners approved IHP amount ÷ approved owners, against NFIP `amountPaidOnBuildingClaim` ÷ paid claims, for the same `eventDesignationNumber`/disaster and county.
- Angle 13: `nonPaymentReasonBuilding`, with codes 98/99 excluded. This already matches the site's rule.
- Angle 14: `buildingDamageAmount` and `amountPaidOnBuildingClaim`, paid claims only.
- Angle 16: `causeOfDamage`. Multiple codes per claim are possible, so state the counting rule.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the HA Owners or Policies v3 data dictionary pages, so exact HA Owners field names (for example the approved IHP amount field) are unverified here. The site's repo may already hold them.

## Q3. Where could each angle mislead readers?

### Takeaway
The three worst traps are the claims `countyCode` caveat, `waterDepth` units, and premium-vs-claims windows that do not match.

### Cited Findings
- Claims `countyCode` is the "primary county associated with the project" and "may not match the property's exact county" ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).
- Latitude and longitude are rounded to one decimal place. Points may fall in the wrong county or state, so use the county fields instead ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).
- `amountPaidOnBuildingClaim` can be negative when a check is reissued ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).
- **`waterDepth` unit conflict.** FEMA's v3 page says inches, with some records in feet ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)). Nature Comms treated depth as feet and found about 5% of records apparently in inches ([Nature Comms 2020](https://www.Nature.Com/articles/s41467-020-15264-2)). These contradict each other, so any "deepest flood" figure is unsafe.
- `ratedFloodZone` is the zone used to rate the policy. `floodZoneCurrent` is the zone on today's map, and the two can differ ([Claims v2/v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/fima-nfip-redacted-claims-v2)).
- Estimates of claims outside the SFHA range from 20% to 45% depending on period and method ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20180910_IN10890_f6d16ecbee2f25ece61efb428997c48ce69e1136.html); [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/node/4024); [InsuranceNewsNet/Neptune](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/flood-risk-doesnt-stop-at-the-100-year-floodplain-line-data-shows)).
- Helene coverage figures range from 0.5% to 5.2% depending on the denominator ([Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2024/10/03/267204.htm); [NC Newsline](https://ncnewsline.com/2025/04/22/few-were-insured-against-helenes-floods-those-that-were-faced-long-claims-frustrating-hurdles/)).
- FEMA disputes tying the policy drop directly to RR2.0, and enrollment was already falling before it ([InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/femas-risk-rating-2-0-is-reshaping-flood-insurance-leaving-many-households-financially-vulnerable-to-growing-flood-risk); [JCRR](https://journalofcrr.com/research/03-07-gourevitch-et-al/)).
- Many grant-vs-payout figures come from FEMA or FloodSmart outreach, which promotes flood insurance ([FloodSmart agents](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/articles/what-your-clients-need-know-about-disaster-assistance-and-flood-insurance)).

### Inferences (risk per angle)
- **Claims outside the SFHA (1):** medium risk. Label the base as "zone used to rate the policy", exclude blanks, and give claims vs dollars separately.
- **Map changed under the claim (12):** medium risk. Today's zone is not the zone at the time of loss, so the line must say "on today's map".
- **RR2.0 drop (2):** high risk if worded as cause. Print the change only and say "why prices change: the data cannot say".
- **Grants vs payout (3):** medium risk. These are different populations (owners who applied vs policyholders who claimed), so label both bases. An IHP grant is not damage.
- **Declarations with no policies (4):** medium risk. Housing units vs policies vs homes; the denominator must be named.
- **Repetitive loss (5):** low-medium risk. The Multiple Loss data is aggregated and the definitions differ (RL vs SRL vs IA multiple-loss).
- **Old maps (6):** medium risk. The effective date is not the study date, and a whole county can contain several panels with different dates.
- **Premium vs claims over decades (7):** **high risk.** Policy premiums are available roughly from 2009 on, while claims go back to 1978. Premium is not total cost (surcharges and fees are separate). Katrina-type single events dominate. Do not publish without matching windows.
- **Water depth (11):** **do not publish extremes.**
- **Not-paid reasons (13), damage-vs-paid gap (14), second-home share (15), cause mix (16):** low-medium risk. Damage is actual cash value and payouts are net of deductible and limits, so the gap is not "money denied".

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the exact first year of premium data in Policies v3. "Roughly 2009" is from the site's prior knowledge, not from a source fetched here.

## Q4. What do the data dictionaries say where it matters?

### Takeaway
Read Claims v3 directly. v2 is gone, and v3 confirms the county caveat, negative payments, the units warning, the cause codes and the non-payment codes.

### Cited Findings
- Claims v3 definitions (`waterDepth`, `countyCode`, cause codes 0-4, 7-9 and A-D, `nonPaymentReasonBuilding` 01-20 plus 97/98/99, `rateMethod` codes including RatingEngine, and `primaryResidenceIndicator` as more than 50% occupancy) ([OpenFEMA Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).
- Cause codes 7 and 8 (erosion) are valid only for losses before 1995-09-23. Codes B, C and D are expedited or remote-adjustment handling flags, not physical causes ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).
- `eventDesignationNumber` has been used since June 2020 and `ficoNumber` before that, so a per-event join must handle both ([Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)).

### Inferences
- In angle 16, codes B, C and D must be kept out of any "cause" chart, or labelled as handling flags.

### Gaps
- The Policies v3, HA Owners, Multiple Loss Properties, NFHL and NOAA sea-level dictionaries were not fetched in this pass.
- No NOAA sea-level angle was checked for novelty beyond one headline, "flood insurance is vanishing where the water is rising fastest" ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/flood-insurance-is-vanishing-where-the-water-is-rising-fastest-591837.aspx)). That suggests the pairing of sea-level rise with policy decline is already covered.
